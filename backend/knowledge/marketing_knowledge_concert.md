## Core Checklist — Concert Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: checklist
-->

**Phase 1 — Booking & Ticket Setup**
- Venue and date confirmed.
- Ticket visuals and ticket sales copy prepared.
  - If `ticket_owner = manager`: manager prepares these.
  - If `ticket_owner = venue`: venue prepares these — confirm the ticket link works before moving on.

**Phase 2 — Announcement & Print Assets**
- Posters/flyers designed and sent to print, once ticket visuals/link are ready. Printing and hanging can happen anytime up to ~1 month before the show — poster wall space is often booked/limited, so this isn't tied tightly to announcement day.
- Facebook event created, with the ticket link included.
- Social media post announcing the show.
  - If `is_paid = true`: post funnels to the ticket link.
  - If `is_paid = false`: post funnels to attendance/RSVP instead.

**Phase 3 — Ongoing Promo**
- Social media posts every few days, continuing between announcement and show date.
  - If `is_paid = true`: continue funneling to tickets.
  - If `is_paid = false`: continue funneling to attendance/RSVP.

**Phase 4 — Day-of / Post**
- Concert day preparation content.
- Post-concert: recap post, and reshares of stories/tags from fans and attendees.

## Strategy: Classic / Safe Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: mid,established
goal: sell_out,build_hype
-->

**Core idea:** Follow the checklist phases in order, no artificial scarcity or urgency tricks — maximize reach through consistent, predictable promotion.

**Sequence:**
- Standard checklist phases 1–4, no shortcuts or reordering.
- Announcement goes out to the full network at once (no soft-launch to a smaller group first).
- Promo posts are steady and even-paced across the whole window, not front-loaded or back-loaded.

**Social media ideas (roughly biweekly rhythm across the promo window):**
- Week 1: announcement post, Facebook event live, poster reveal if ready.
- Week 2: lineup/support-act spotlight (if applicable), venue spotlight (why this venue).
- Week 3: ticket reminder post, behind-the-scenes rehearsal or prep clip.
- Final week: countdown story, "last chance" reminder if paid, final lineup/setlist teaser.
- Day-of: arrival/soundcheck story.
- Post-show: recap reel, fan reshares.

**If artist size allows (established acts):** larger poster runs in high-footfall areas, a short press release to local outlets, one radio mention if there's an existing relationship with a station.

**Best fit:** established or steadily-growing acts with an existing audience who don't need to manufacture urgency — the show sells on reputation.
**Doesn't fit:** brand-new acts with no existing following — there's nothing for "reach" to actually reach.

## Strategy: Hype / FOMO Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: emerging,mid
goal: build_hype,sell_out
-->

**Core idea:** Manufacture scarcity and social proof early, so the show feels like something people need to act on immediately rather than something they'll get around to.

**Sequence:**
- Soft-announce to core fans/mailing list only, before the public Facebook event goes live.
- Optional short exclusive pre-sale window for that inner circle, with a hard cap on quantity.
- Public announcement only happens once a meaningful chunk of tickets are already gone (signals momentum, not desperation).
- Ongoing promo leans on "X% sold" messaging rather than plain reminders.

**Social media ideas:**
- Pre-announcement: cryptic teaser post/story (no date, just "something's coming").
- Soft-announce: exclusive early-access story to close fans only.
- Public announce: "X% sold in Y hours" post.
- Mid-window: fan reaction/excitement reposts, countdown stickers.
- Final week: "almost sold out" or "last tickets" framing (only if genuinely true — false scarcity backfires if fans catch it).
- Post-show: "you should've been there" recap, feeding the next show's FOMO.

**Best fit:** mid-size acts with a genuinely engaged core fanbase that can drive a real pre-sale — the tactic depends on authentic early demand, not fabricated numbers.
**Doesn't fit:** very large venues where you need volume fast, or acts without an existing core group to soft-launch to.

## Strategy: Community-First Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: emerging,mid
goal: community,build_hype
city_type: secondary
-->

**Core idea:** Build outward from the fan club and local scene before spending on broad reach — the show is sold by people, not ads.

**Sequence:**
- Fan club / mailing list gets the news first, with a personal message rather than a template post.
- Local scene outreach — other local artists, local promoters, community pages — before general public announcement.
- Street team (friends, fans) shares physical or digital assets personally, not just reposts.
- Public announcement comes after the local base is already activated.

**Social media ideas:**
- Personal video message to fans announcing the show (more effective than a static post here).
- "Tag someone you're bringing" style engagement post.
- Local-scene collab posts — cross-promotion with other artists/venues in the area.
- Street team content: fans posting their own "I'm going" stories, reshared by the artist.
- Post-show: spotlight fans/attendees by name or story reshare, reinforcing the community feeling for next time.

**Best fit:** regional/local acts building a scene, secondary-city shows where there's no large existing media apparatus to lean on.
**Doesn't fit:** large-scale shows needing volume beyond what a local scene can realistically supply.

## Strategy: PR / Media-Led Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: established
goal: press,sell_out
-->

**Core idea:** Lead with press and media presence, let ticket-buying follow from visibility rather than from social posts alone.

**Sequence:**
- Press release drafted and sent to relevant outlets ahead of public announcement.
- Interview outreach — radio, and TV if the artist's profile supports it — timed to land in the weeks before the show.
- Public social announcement follows or coincides with press coverage, so posts can link to/reference the press mentions.
- Ongoing promo period leans on resharing press coverage as social proof.

**Social media ideas:**
- "As seen on / heard on [outlet]" posts once coverage lands.
- Interview clip reposts (radio/TV soundbites cut for social).
- Countdown posts referencing press attention ("the show everyone's talking about").
- Final week: standard ticket reminder, now backed by the press narrative built up.

**If artist size allows:** this is the tier where digital screen ads, larger-format posters, and paid press placements make sense — these only work if there's enough existing profile for press/media to bite.
**Best fit:** established artists with enough of a profile that press outlets will actually respond.
**Doesn't fit:** brand-new or very small acts — press outreach without a hook usually gets ignored, and the budget is better spent elsewhere at that stage.

## Strategy: Low-Budget Guerrilla Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: emerging
budget: low
goal: build_hype,community
-->

**Core idea:** No ad spend — organic social, personal networks, and cheap physical presence do the work.

**Sequence:**
- Skip paid promotion entirely; every checklist phase relies on free channels.
- Posters/flyers kept simple and cheap, hand-distributed rather than professionally placed.
- Heavy reliance on personal asks — the artist and friends directly messaging people, not just posting publicly.

**Social media ideas:**
- Raw, unpolished behind-the-scenes content — rehearsal clips, poster-hanging footage — authenticity over production value.
- Direct "share this if you're coming" asks.
- Collab posts with any other small local acts on the bill, doubling reach for free.
- Day-of: simple story reminders, no paid boosting.

**Best fit:** brand-new or very early-stage acts with no marketing budget — the honesty of a low-budget rollout can itself be part of the appeal.
**Doesn't fit:** any show needing to sell beyond a small room — this tactic has a low ceiling by design.

## Strategy: VIP / Experience-Heavy Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: mid,established
budget: medium,high
goal: sell_out
-->

**Core idea:** Sell an experience, not just a ticket — tiered pricing and added extras justify a higher price point and create their own promotional hook.

**Sequence:**
- Ticket visuals/copy (Phase 1) need to define tiers up front — standard vs. VIP (meet & greet, early entry, exclusive merch) — before announcement.
- Announcement highlights the VIP tier alongside standard tickets, not as an afterthought.
- Ongoing promo splits messaging: standard-tier posts drive volume, VIP-tier posts drive higher-margin sales to the most engaged fans.

**Social media ideas:**
- "What's included in VIP" explainer post/story.
- Merch preview exclusive to VIP buyers.
- Countdown content teasing the meet & greet or exclusive extras.
- Post-show: VIP experience recap (separate from general recap) — reinforces value for the next show's VIP tier.

**Best fit:** acts with a fanbase willing to pay a premium for closeness/exclusivity — usually mid-to-established acts with a dedicated core.
**Doesn't fit:** brand-new acts without enough of a devoted following to justify a premium tier, or budget-conscious audiences.

## Strategy: Festival / Multi-Date Style
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: mid,established
city_type: capital,secondary,festival_city
goal: sell_out,build_hype
-->

**Core idea:** When touring multiple dates, cross-promote across shows rather than treating each date as an isolated rollout.

**Sequence:**
- All dates announced together as a tour/run, even if ticket links go live on different days.
- Each city's promo posts reference the full run ("also playing X, Y, Z"), pulling audience from nearby dates into whichever is easiest for them to attend.
- Local specifics (venue, ticket owner, paid/free) still tracked per date via each date's own project row — the shared calendar handles this naturally.

**Social media ideas:**
- One "full run" announcement graphic listing all dates/cities.
- Per-city posts closer to each date, but always mentioning the other dates.
- "Which city are you catching us in?" engagement post.
- Recap content after each show can be reused to build hype for the next city on the run.

**Best fit:** any act touring multiple dates in a region — even emerging acts benefit from cross-promotion here, since the effort is shared across dates.
**Doesn't fit:** single, isolated shows with no other dates to cross-promote against.

## Strategy: Last-Minute / Urgency Rollout
<!--
chunk_type: strategy
artist_size: emerging,mid,established
phase: 2_weeks,1_week
goal: sell_out,build_hype
-->

**Core idea:** When there's only 2–3 weeks (the checklist's minimum viable window), compress phases and prioritize the highest-impact actions only.

**Sequence:**
- Ticket setup and announcement happen almost simultaneously — no soft-launch window, no waiting for perfect assets.
- Skip printed posters entirely if there's not enough lead time to print and hang them usefully.
- Promo frequency increases immediately (near-daily) instead of the normal every-few-days rhythm, since there's no time to spread it out.

**Social media ideas:**
- Announcement post is also the first ticket-push post — no separate reveal/sell split.
- Daily countdown stories from day one.
- Reshare anything fans post immediately — no time to wait and curate.
- Direct personal asks (DMs, group chats) alongside public posts, since organic reach alone may not have time to build.

**Best fit:** any act suddenly given a short-notice slot (support act added late, venue opened up) — this is about damage control on timing, not a preferred strategy.
**Doesn't fit:** never a first choice when more lead time is available — this variant exists purely for when the calendar forces it.
